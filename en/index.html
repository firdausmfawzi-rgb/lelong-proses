What is an auction property?

An auction property is a home sold at an open auction by a bank. It is usually auctioned because the current owner has failed to pay the bank's monthly instalments for several months, so the bank sells it to recover what is still owed on the loan.

Why is it auctioned?

When the owner misses the monthly instalments for several months, the bank may sell the home at auction to cover what is still owed. The starting price, the reserve price, is set by the bank.

Why are there still few buyers?

To qualify for the auction, a buyer needs a 10% deposit. Most buyers cannot easily set this aside, so there is little competition. That is the opening for buyers who can.

Why are so many afraid?

Many have heard bad stories: haunted homes, badly damaged homes, suicide cases and so on. In truth, most people only hear about the bad experiences. These stories keep buyers away.

A gold mine for investors. For investors who already buy at auction, this market is a gold mine. Homes 30% or more below market value are easier to find at auction, and some units sell at 50% or more below the bank value.
Why it pays

The advantages of buying at auction

An auction property is not only cheap. Bought the right way, it can bring in a high return every month.

Buy below market value

The reserve price is often well below market value, especially after the first auction finds no buyer. You start with a gain on day one.

High ROI, rent covers the instalment

Because the price is low, the monthly rent is often enough, or more than enough, to cover the bank's monthly instalment. Most bank auction units can bring in extra cash flow.

Renovation and furniture left behind

Some homes are already renovated, and some previous owners leave furniture behind. The buyer may get all of it with the home.

Before you bid

Getting ready before you buy

An auction property is one of the investments with the highest returns, but it comes with its own risks. The right preparation is your best protection.

Check your loan eligibility first

Before you bid, check your loan eligibility with a bank. Ask your agent: the agent has a banker and a lawyer who are experienced in auction deals.

Choose an experienced team

The agent, the lawyer and the banker each bring their own expertise to help you before you decide. Choosing a team experienced in auctions matters a great deal.

If the loan is not approved

Remember: if your loan is not approved, your deposit may be forfeited and used to pay the arrears of the owner whose home was auctioned. That is why the loan must be checked first.

Check with me first. I have a banker and a lawyer experienced in auction deals to check your eligibility before you bid.
Not just the deposit

Be ready for the previous owner's arrears too

Besides the 10% deposit, the buyer must also be ready to pay all of the previous owner's arrears after winning the auction, usually once the loan is approved. The arrears that usually need paying:

a. Maintenance fee

Owed to the building management body.

b. Quit rent

Owed to the land office.

c. Assessment tax

Owed to the local council.

d. IWK bill

Owed to Indah Water for sewerage.

e. Water bill

Owed for the water supply.

f. Developer arrears

Owed to the developer, if any.

Some can be claimed back. Part of these arrears can be claimed back from the bank. Ask the agent you appoint which ones.
Once you are ready

Register for the auction through a bank panel agent

Once your loan eligibility is checked and the arrears are counted, you can go ahead and register for the auction.

Register through a bank panel agent
